# Frozen protocol: finite synthetic FCFS service-delay amplification

Frozen 2026-09-08 before any platform confirmation run. Investigator runtime verified gpt-6-astra in session turn_context. All inputs are original deterministic synthetic records, released under repository AGPL-3.0-or-later. No patient data, physiology coupling, or clinical interpretation.

Question: how does adding one second to every service change waiting and completion in finite queues across offered load, burst timing, heterogeneous service and server count? Primary endpoints are paired mean wait and makespan differences; secondary endpoints are p95 wait, max wait, total busy time and sample-path queue area. Hypothesis: delay near capacity produces accumulation well beyond one second; low nominal load does not protect bursty workloads from substantial waits. This is a bounded engineering benchmark, not a stochastic population inference.

Confirmatory design: 800 jobs per case; two preselected seeds 1701 and 2903; six scenarios: smooth one-server offered load 0.50, 0.95, 1.10; smooth four-server load 0.95; batches of 40 four-server load 0.70; heterogeneous four-server load 0.70. Smooth services uniform 8..12, arrival gaps uniform 0.8..1.2 times nominal interval; durations and gaps normalized to their exact intended means. Burst services exactly 10; batch spacing 40*10/(4*0.7). Heterogeneous services exactly 640 short jobs of 2 s and 160 long jobs of 42 s, shuffled by seed, regular arrivals 10/(4*0.7). Smooth/burst seeds are retained even where burst realizes identical inputs. Paired delays 0 and 1 s; same jobs in each pair. Arrival starts at 0; no warmup removal. Nominal load = mean service/(servers*nominal mean gap), with batch rate convention for bursts; finite realized utilization is separately reported as busy work/(servers*makespan). Delayed nominal load is baseline times 1.1.

Independent verification uses a sorted workload vector without heap scheduling to compute wait/start/finish; one-server cases additionally use Lindley waiting recursion. Server identities are checked against per-server finish histories with earliest prior finish then smallest server id on equal finish; ties do not mean smallest among every currently idle server. Invariants: nonnegative wait, no overlap, exact job count/order, finish-start equals service+delay, summed duration equals busy work, queue-event integration equals sum waits, reported summary equals CSV recomputation. Numeric tolerance 1e-8 s absolute + 1e-12 relative. CSV must round-trip numerically and report/model card/runtime/manifest are inspected through the actual CLI.

Held-out cases, generated only after main cases: (1) reversed input order of smooth-low seed1701, requiring byte-identical schedule; (2) 32 simultaneous reverse-order ASCII ids on 16 servers, service 0.001; (3) three one-server jobs with service/arrival boundaries (0.001,10000; arrival 1e6) and delay100; (4) two-server idle-gap and case-sensitive ASCII tie example distinguishing earliest-free from smallest-currently-idle dispatch. Rejection probes: duplicate ids at seal; non-ASCII id at seal; changed sealed delay at validate. Rejections are expected evidence, not exclusions.

Budget: 28 sequential run calls, <=2000 jobs per call; 32 absolute run ceiling only for clearly documented mechanical recovery, no endpoint-driven exploration. 60 s worker timeout per run. Stop and report if immutable build changes, worker fails, check mismatch or deadline exceeds 15 minutes; preserve every command outcome. No selective reruns, scenario deletion or post-result hypothesis change. CLI example -> edited draft -> seal -> run -> inspect is mandatory. study.py is frozen by SHA256 before execution alongside this protocol; generated inputs/results and command-level inventory retain all successful, rejected and failed attempts. No new paid resources or external writes.

Uncertainty: report both seed effects and their finite range; no confidence interval, p-value, clinical effect, steady-state claim or empirical generalization. Two seeds do not estimate tail or population uncertainty; burst duplicate seeds add no replication. Algorithmic comparator shares specification but not platform implementation; invariants can share conceptual mistakes. Primary source background: D. V. Lindley (1952), The theory of queues with a single server, Mathematical Proceedings of the Cambridge Philosophical Society 48(2), 277–289, https://doi.org/10.1017/S0305004100027638 (publisher abstract accessed 2026-09-08). The explicit finite recurrence used here is stated in the manuscript; no inaccessible full-text claim.
